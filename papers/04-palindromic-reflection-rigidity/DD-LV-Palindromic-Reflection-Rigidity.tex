\begin{abstract}
We study the rational reflection that a palindrome $p$ defines for the two Cohn matrices. We
classify the finite words whose terminal columns it takes to other word columns: they are exactly
the concatenations of the blocks $ab\,p$ and $ba\,p$, which it exchanges. Hence two palindromes have
equal Gram labels and a root midpoint at the ratio of the centre exactly when they are exchanged
around it. Such pairs have nonprimitive completed letter counts, so no two central words collide at
such a midpoint; an explicit quadruple shows that this hypothesis cannot be dropped. Exchanges
around nested centres compose into cubes of palindromes with one label. In the Thue--Morse coding of
Reutenauer and Vuillon, the Gram label of a palindrome is the length of an explicit Christoffel word
and the Gram root its number of letters $b$; for Markov words, uniqueness holds if and only if equal
labels force directing words of equal length. An exact enumeration of all palindromes with label
below $10^{26}$ finds no coincidence of labels that exchanges do not produce. Beyond it, two
palindromes of $52$ letters share a label without being joined by exchanges, yet both are built
around the empty word; we conjecture that any two palindromes with the same label are built around
a common palindrome, which would imply Frobenius--Markov uniqueness. Away from the midpoint we derive
the two-sided reflection equation and a fourth-power bound on the common-ancestor label under
bounded parent imbalance. The uniqueness conjecture itself remains open.
\end{abstract}
