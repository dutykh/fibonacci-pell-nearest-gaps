\begin{abstract}
The Frobenius--Markov uniqueness conjecture asserts that a positive integer solution of the Markov
equation is determined by its largest entry. Through the Cohn matrices, every binary palindrome has
a label, a positive integer, and a root, a square root of minus one modulo the label; central words
have Markov numbers as labels, and the conjecture says that distinct central words have distinct
labels. We prove that the rational reflection defined by a palindrome maps the column of a finite
word, the image of a fixed vector under its Cohn matrix, to that of another word, even up to a
scalar, exactly when the first word is a concatenation of blocks, each formed by \emph{ab} or
\emph{ba} followed by the palindrome, and the second is obtained by exchanging \emph{ab} and
\emph{ba} in every block. Hence two distinct palindromes with the same label have roots, divided by
that label, symmetric about the root of a palindrome divided by its label exactly when they are
built around that palindrome, as copies of it separated by \emph{ab} or \emph{ba}, and exchanged; no
two central words form such a pair, and nested exchanges give cubes of palindromes with one label.
In the Thue--Morse coding of Reutenauer and Vuillon, the label is the length of an explicit
Christoffel word and the root its number of letters \emph{b}. Among palindromes with label at most
$10^{26}$, all coincidences of labels come from exchanges; beyond, two palindromes of 52 letters
share a label without being joined by exchanges, though both are built around the empty word. We
conjecture that any two palindromes with the same label are built around a common palindrome, which
would imply the uniqueness conjecture.
\end{abstract}
