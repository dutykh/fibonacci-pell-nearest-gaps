\begin{abstract}
We study equal traces of primitive simple curves on the modular
once-punctured torus, with the relative occurrence count fixed and the
integral Markov character allowed to vary. For every occurrence count we
give a complete decision procedure, polynomial in that count, together
with an exact test for surface isometry. The proof combines positive
matrix paths with a controlled comparison between actual Markov triples
and commuting trace triples: positive quotient mutation bounds the error
at positive product index, while at minimum index mechanical balance and
spectral coordinates control its propagation along the Euclidean
algorithm. Both parameter bounds are analytic, valid over all positive
integral characters, and are stated explicitly in the text. An exact
finite computation classifies all primitive occurrence families with
between two and six occurrences; every equal pair in these families is
isometric. Two further arguments give the same conclusion for every pair
of intersection number at most twenty-four, so that a nonisometric
equality would need intersection at least twenty-five. These results
leave the occurrence count unbounded and do not resolve Markov
uniqueness.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Supplementary material}
The supplementary material accompanying this article contains the exact computations used in the proofs
of \cref{thm:small-occurrences,thm:small-intersections} and
\cref{prop:intersection-nineteen,prop:intersection-twentythree}: the
equality records with their isometry witnesses, the residue cover, the
polynomial coefficient lists, and instructions for repeating the
verification.
